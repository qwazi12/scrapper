# Explicit authorized background audio in native videos

Short/Reel beats can select an indexed authorized audio asset separately from the
visual. This is optional; no music is selected automatically, and the visual
video's soundtrack remains excluded. Image, carousel and manual treatments reject
background-audio choices instead of implying unsupported publishing capabilities.

The owner supplies every choice: source ID, 0.25–12 second interval within its
indexed duration, relative level from −36 to −12 dB, and loop or finish-then-silence
policy. Incomplete choices, invalid bounds/levels, wrong asset kinds and missing
current source bytes/permissions fail closed. The source preview is the indexed
file, before the final mix. No source is downloaded or generated by this feature.

For a campaign using background audio, all narration stems target the same
−18 LUFS reference before mixing, including beats without background. The selected
background stem targets that reference before its explicit level reduction. It
fades at the beat boundary; a shorter excerpt either loops or leaves background
silence as chosen. Narration retains its complete readable-duration treatment.
The final mix still uses native export normalization and decode/stream/duration/
loudness/peak inspection. These are target policies and measured gates, not proof
of speech intelligibility, excellent music selection or subjective balance.
Automatic speech ducking and seam-aware musical loop matching are not implemented.

The manifest retains original/normalized source provenance via its frozen inputs,
the exact normalized source hash, source interval, actual retained audio-excerpt
hash/duration, presentation duration, level and ending/mix policy. All selected
sources and the output root remain under renewable shared claims through rendering
and promotion. Inputs are checked after claim acquisition and before promotion;
a changed source cannot produce a successful export row. Retained audio excerpts
join exact review/queue/publication integrity checks. Conservative reference
tracking already walks these manifest file entries and the catalog's source rows.

Every selected background track produces its own exact-acceptance finding, even
when machine inspection has no warnings. The owner must listen to the complete
mixed export, assess masking/repetition/cuts, and record a substantive decision
and rights/intended-use attestation. The app has not verified licenses or granted
publication authority. Rendering or editorial acceptance never publishes.

Native browser recovery includes the audio source/range/level/ending choices.
They remain private through reload, failed requests and changed research; late
responses cannot erase a newer editor's draft. Unsupported format combinations
remain blocked after recovery. The render/queue/source-fingerprint policies are
unchanged by restoring local words.

Local contracts use original750Hz narration and1100Hz background tones, plus a
separate1900Hz video soundtrack in the combined case. Actual final-file frequency
measurements check foreground dominance, loop-versus-silence behavior, voice-level
consistency across beats and exclusion of the video soundtrack. Tests also cover
strict inputs, missing/changed source ownership, retained-excerpt corruption and
mandatory background review. This is synthetic signal evidence, not human speech
or musical-quality proof. Actual UI verification imports original local video and
audio, selects both ranges/levels, renders, plays/seeks, records both review
findings and creates only a review-queue entry. The progress ledger records exact
completed results and failures before they are counted.

Remaining work includes deeper native recuts/Reel composition, semantic asset/music
retrieval, structured rights/attribution revisions, seamless musical transitions,
word alignment and excellent real-content editorial examples. No paid service,
public upload/reply, live account change or deferred source-channel archive is
required for this local feature.
